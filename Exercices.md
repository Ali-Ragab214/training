# Task: Create a Beginner Home Workout — Legs, Glutes, Calves & Abs

You are a fitness workout-planning assistant and a workout-content data designer for a fitness website.

Create one complete workout session for a female beginner who is new to resistance training. The workout must be suitable for home use and require no gym equipment. A stable chair or wall may be used for balance.

## 1. Workout Metadata

- Workout name: Beginner Legs, Glutes, Calves & Abs
    
- Level: Beginner
    
- Location: Home
    
- Target audience: Female beginner
    
- Estimated duration: 30–40 minutes
    
- Equipment: Exercise mat (optional), stable chair or wall for support
    
- Main muscle groups: Quadriceps, hamstrings, gluteus maximus, gluteus medius, calves and abdominal/core muscles
    
- Workout objective: Learn correct exercise technique, build foundational strength, improve balance and gradually develop training consistency.
    

## 2. Exercise Order and Details

### Exercise 1: Warm-up & Dynamic Mobility

- Duration: 5–7 minutes
    
- March in Place: 60 seconds
    
- Arm Circles: 30 seconds
    
- Hip Circles: 30 seconds
    
- Supported Leg Swings: 10 repetitions per leg
    
- Easy Bodyweight Squats: 8 repetitions
    
- Purpose: Gradually increase body temperature and prepare the hips, knees and ankles for exercise.
    
- Instructions: Perform movements gently and without pain. Do not use aggressive static stretching before the workout.
    

### Exercise 2: Bodyweight Squat

- Exercise type: Compound
    
- Target muscles: Quadriceps and glutes
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 10–12
    
- Rest: 60–90 seconds between sets
    
- Equipment: None; chair may be used as a depth target.
    
- Instructions:
    
    1. Stand with feet approximately shoulder-width apart.
        
    2. Brace the core and push the hips backward while bending the knees.
        
    3. Lower to a comfortable depth while keeping the feet grounded.
        
    4. Keep the knees aligned with the toes.
        
    5. Push through the feet to return to standing.
        
- Common mistakes: Knees collapsing inward, heels lifting, losing balance or descending beyond a comfortable range.
    
- Easier variation: Chair Squat.
    

### Exercise 3: Glute Bridge

- Exercise type: Glute-focused strength exercise
    
- Target muscles: Glutes and hamstrings
    
- Sets: 3
    
- Reps: 12–15
    
- Rest: 60 seconds between sets
    
- Equipment: Exercise mat, optional
    
- Instructions:
    
    1. Lie on the back with knees bent and feet flat on the floor.
        
    2. Keep arms relaxed beside the body.
        
    3. Brace the core and lift the hips by contracting the glutes.
        
    4. Stop when the torso and thighs form an approximately straight line.
        
    5. Squeeze the glutes briefly at the top.
        
    6. Lower the hips slowly with control.
        
- Common mistakes: Excessively arching the lower back, pushing through the neck, or lifting the hips too high.
    
- Easier variation: Short-range Glute Bridge.
    

### Exercise 4: Donkey Kicks

- Exercise type: Unilateral glute exercise
    
- Target muscles: Glutes
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 10–12 per leg
    
- Rest: 45–60 seconds after completing both legs
    
- Equipment: Exercise mat, optional
    
- Instructions:
    
    1. Start on all fours with hands under shoulders and knees under hips.
        
    2. Keep the core engaged and the spine neutral.
        
    3. Keep one knee bent and lift that leg backward and upward.
        
    4. Stop before the lower back starts arching or the pelvis rotates.
        
    5. Lower the knee slowly and repeat.
        
    6. Complete all repetitions on one leg, then switch sides.
        
- Common mistakes: Rotating the pelvis, swinging the leg, or arching the lower back.
    
- Easier variation: Smaller range of motion.
    

### Exercise 5: Supported Reverse Lunge

- Exercise type: Unilateral compound exercise
    
- Target muscles: Quadriceps and glutes
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 8 per leg
    
- Rest: 60–90 seconds between sets
    
- Equipment: Stable chair or wall for balance
    
- Instructions:
    
    1. Stand upright and hold a stable support if needed.
        
    2. Step one foot backward.
        
    3. Bend both knees slightly and lower only as far as comfortable.
        
    4. Keep the front foot flat and the front knee aligned with the toes.
        
    5. Push through the front foot to return to standing.
        
    6. Alternate legs or complete one side before switching.
        
- Common mistakes: Losing balance, taking an excessively long step, or forcing a deep range of motion.
    
- Easier variation: Supported Split Squat with a short range of motion.
    
- Safety note: If the beginner cannot perform this movement comfortably, replace it with Chair Squats.
    

### Exercise 6: Standing Calf Raises

- Exercise type: Isolation exercise
    
- Target muscles: Gastrocnemius and soleus
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 12–15
    
- Rest: 45–60 seconds between sets
    
- Equipment: Stable chair or wall for balance
    
- Instructions:
    
    1. Stand upright with feet approximately hip-width apart.
        
    2. Hold the support lightly.
        
    3. Raise both heels slowly while keeping the body upright.
        
    4. Pause briefly at the top.
        
    5. Lower the heels slowly and under control.
        
- Common mistakes: Bouncing, rushing repetitions or leaning heavily on the support.
    
- Easier variation: Smaller range of motion.
    

### Exercise 7: Dead Bug

- Exercise type: Core stability exercise
    
- Target muscles: Abdominals and deep core muscles
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 8–10 per side
    
- Rest: 45–60 seconds between sets
    
- Equipment: Exercise mat, optional
    
- Instructions:
    
    1. Lie on the back with arms extended toward the ceiling.
        
    2. Raise the legs with hips and knees bent approximately 90 degrees.
        
    3. Gently brace the abdominal muscles.
        
    4. Slowly extend one arm and the opposite leg.
        
    5. Return to the starting position and alternate sides.
        
    6. Maintain a stable lower back throughout the movement.
        
- Common mistakes: Arching the lower back, moving too quickly or extending the limbs beyond the available control.
    
- Easier variation: Move one heel at a time while keeping the other foot supported.
    

### Exercise 8: Cool-down & Gentle Stretching

- Duration: 3–5 minutes
    
- Activities:
    
    - Slow marching or walking in place.
        
    - Gentle quadriceps stretch.
        
    - Gentle hamstring stretch.
        
    - Gentle glute stretch.
        
- Hold each stretch for approximately 15–25 seconds without bouncing.
    
- Do not force a stretch or continue through pain.

### Exercise 2: Wall Push-ups

- Order: 2
    
- Category: Chest / Compound
    
- Difficulty: Beginner
    
- Target muscles: Chest
    
- Secondary muscles: Triceps and anterior shoulders
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 8–12
    
- Rest: 60–90 seconds
    

Starting position:  
Stand facing a wall, approximately an arm's length away. Place both palms against the wall at chest height, slightly wider than shoulder-width.

Execution:

1. Keep the body aligned from head to heels.
    
2. Bend the elbows and bring the chest toward the wall.
    
3. Keep the elbows at a comfortable angle rather than flaring them excessively.
    
4. Push through the palms to return to the starting position.
    
5. Repeat with controlled movement.
    

Common mistakes:

- Arching the lower back.
    
- Moving only the head toward the wall.
    
- Performing repetitions too quickly.
    
- Allowing the shoulders to shrug toward the ears.
    

Easier variation:

### Exercise 3: Prone W Raises

- Order: 3
    
- Category: Back / Upper-Back Stability
    
- Difficulty: Beginner
    
- Target muscles: Mid-back and scapular stabilizers
    
- Secondary muscles: Rear shoulders
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 8–12
    
- Rest: 45–60 seconds
    

Starting position:  
Lie face down on a comfortable surface. Bend the elbows so the arms form a W shape beside the head, with the thumbs pointing upward when comfortable.

Execution:

1. Keep the neck neutral and look toward the floor.
    
2. Gently draw the shoulder blades together.
    
3. Lift the hands and elbows slightly off the floor without forcing the movement.
    
4. Hold for approximately one second.
    
5. Lower the arms slowly.
    

Common mistakes:

- Lifting the chest excessively.
    
- Overarching the lower back.
    
- Shrugging the shoulders.
    
- Forcing the arms higher than comfortable.
    

Easier variation:  
Perform the movement without lifting the arms off the floor; focus on gently squeezing the shoulder blades together.

- Category: Biceps / Self-Resistance
    
- Difficulty: Beginner
    
- Target muscles: Biceps
    
- Equipment: None
    
- Sets: 2
    
- Reps: 8–10 per arm
    
- Rest: 45–60 seconds
    

Starting position:  
Stand or sit comfortably. Keep one elbow close to the torso and place the opposite hand over the working hand or wrist.

Execution:

1. Begin with the working arm extended comfortably.

4. Control the resistance rather than forcing the working arm.
    
5. Lower the arm slowly while maintaining gentle resistance.
    
6. Switch arms.
    

Common mistakes:

- Using excessive resistance.
    
- Moving the elbow excessively.
    
- Holding the breath.
    
- Creating wrist or elbow discomfort.
    

Easier variation:  
Use very light resistance from the opposite hand and perform a smaller range of motion.

## 3. Session Rules

- Estimated duration: 30–40 minutes, including rest periods.
    
- The user should finish each set with good technique and some repetitions still in reserve.
    
- Rest longer whenever needed to recover.
    
- Beginner users may perform fewer repetitions if necessary.
    
- Increase repetitions gradually only when the current variation is comfortable.
    
- Do not increase the difficulty of multiple exercises simultaneously.
    
- Stop immediately if sharp pain, dizziness, or unusual symptoms occur.
    
- Exercises must never be presented as guaranteed methods of spot fat reduction.

## 6. Accuracy Requirements

The app must honestly describe what these exercises train.

Bodyweight exercises without external resistance cannot reproduce every movement pattern or provide the same loading potential as gym exercises.

In particular:

- Prone W Raises and Reverse Snow Angels are primarily upper-back and shoulder-stability exercises.
    
- Self-Resisted Biceps Curls provide adjustable resistance but cannot be loaded as precisely as conventional curls.
    
- Wall Push-ups and Close-Grip Wall Push-ups train overlapping muscle groups.
    

Present this as a beginner introduction to upper-body training without equipment, not a complete replacement for a progressive resistance-training program.
